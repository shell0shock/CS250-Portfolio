## <ins>How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?</ins>
`I interpret user needs and implement them into a program through clear and deliberate communication with the user, the best way to get the requirements you need can be through casual conversation and direct feedback; talk with the users or have interviews, surveys, look at the data and the usage. Take anything that seems vague and prod at it, don't make assumptions but directly ask specific questions as this can lead to insightful conversation that wasn't planned, you can learn a lot through a 5 minute conversation. The user's needs are the core of your deliverable, it doesn't matter if the product is insufferable for you to use as long as it's exactly what the client wants, sometimes looking through our perspective as the developer can cloud our intent and the user's needs may fall to the wayside, put yourself in the user's shoes, ask why on anything and everything, often times the why gives you the how. `

`User stories help with this immensely as it shifts away from technical jargon and documentation and focuses on actual user interaction and requests, sometimes the client or user doesn't know what to ask for until they've had time to test things out, see what they like and don't like, the user stories helps make clear goals and feedback that enables developers to make meaningful changes that add value and solve a problem, user stories lay out who wants the feature, why and what it's purpose is; it gives you exactly what you need to stay on the right track`



## <ins>How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?</ins>

`I approach developing a program through deliberate documentation and planning, I like to draw out diagrams and flows of actions and processes to see how things connect or overlap, building features iteratively after breaking the main idea out into the smallest possible items that can be done quickly. The main goal is that it works first, than I can go back and make it pretty. Testing is also crucial for developing programs, you never know just what a user may do with the program or how it will break, so you want to test as much as possible and as often as you can on any and every use case, test driven development is extremely useful in this aspect. After large iterations or portions of the program are working or usable you want to then review everything from the ground up, have a sprint retrospective with yourself and see what worked and what didn't, how your approach could change or how you could have done something better. Hindsight's 20/20 but I wear glasses so often a close and in depth look at what you've done from start to now can reveal a lot you didn't expect. `


## <ins>What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?</ins>

`Being a good team member in software development falls down to a few things, communication, feedback and reliability. You need to be able to talk honestly and frequently on how things are going on your end as well as with others, you can't hide blockers or hope something will get resolved; bad news doesn't get better with age. Frequent and clear communication is imperative to being  a good teammate on a software team, there's no point in being on a team if you're not going to work and talk with your team. Feedback and criticism is also extremely important for success on a software team and being a good teammate, you need to be able to take criticism as feedback and advice and not take it personally, nobody wants you to fail and nobody is giving you feedback for the sake of it, they're doing so to help you and the team out. Being able to give and receive feedback is crucial for a teams success, without it nobody will ever grow and improve. Reliability is number one for me personally, you need to be able to hold yourself accountable and deliver what you said you will when you said you will, otherwise you lose your credibility and trust crumbles. Don't fluff expetations jsut to look good, if the task is going to take you a long time be honeset, it's better for everybody involved if you your estimates take mroe time than the delivery, that provides more time for everybody else to refine, test, work on the backlog, etc.`
